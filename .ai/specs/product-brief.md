## Problems, with evidence

- A developer hits a specific blocker with nobody to ask, and it costs between half a day and a week. Three independent accounts: "half a day, and I still don't trust that code" (release next day, the only senior on leave); a Friday Helm upgrade that broke staging, ended in a rollback and slipped a Monday release to Tuesday; an under-priced estimate that cost "about a week of own time". `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-08-20-interview-working-dev.md`, `2026-09-01-interview-backend-dev-fintech.md`, `2026-09-02-interview-freelancer-react.md`
- A fourth developer lost the same half day and is a counter-case, not support: he had someone to ask ("to bym napisał na wewnętrznym, zawsze ktoś jest. jest nas dwieście osób"), refused to pay ("za to? nie. nic się nie paliło") and solved it himself. `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-09-02-interview-platform-dev-solves-alone.md`
- Asking strangers in a community is slow and often silent: 109 new help threads in 14 days, 23 of them (21%) never got a human reply; among the 86 answered the median wait for a first reply was 67 minutes and only 31 got one inside 30 minutes. `[DATA]` `2026-09-04-discord-help-requests.csv`, six `#help-*` channels of a ~4,000-member Discord, 2026-08-18 to 2026-08-31, bot and job posts removed
- Urgency buys nothing there: 31 of the 109 threads carried an urgency signal and 9 of those 31 got no reply at all; for the urgent threads that were answered the median first reply was 64.5 minutes. The signal is softer than the phrases suggest — the modal value is the label "deadline mentioned" (14), then "deploy today" (5), "release tomorrow" (4), "prod is down" (4), "client waiting" (2), "demo in 2h" (2), so only 12 of the 31 are one of the four vivid phrases, in a column meant to hold the poster's own words. `[DATA]` same file
- Most threads do not end solved: 35 ended with the poster saying it was solved, 20 partially, 45 unresolved and 9 unknown. `[DATA]` same file — "resolved" means only that the poster said so. These counts and the 67-minute median are re-derived from the CSV for this brief; the reading aid in `2026-09-04-data-request-discord-help.md` says 68 minutes and 23 partial and is wrong on both
- The paid alternatives fail on the two things that matter to this moment — time-to-a-person and the medium. One developer spent 40 minutes finding someone on Codementor and then lost the session to a broken microphone, and did not go back — "i tyle, przeszło mi. nie przez niego", which is losing interest rather than blaming the mentor; "would not return" is founder A's summary inference, not his words. `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-09-01-interview-backend-dev-fintech.md` · Codementor's public React listing showed rates of $15 to $80 per 15 minutes on 10 cards and promised no response time. `[BENCHMARK]` https://www.codementor.io/react-experts, checked 2026-09-04
- Trust is judged by public work, not by scores. In the survey, "what they have built or written" was chosen 41 times of 54, a personal recommendation 29, response time 27, and ratings 12 — the least-chosen signal. `[DATA]` `2026-09-04-survey-discord-willingness-to-pay.md`, 54 responses, one Discord, 2026-08-29 to 2026-09-03
- Ratings carry no information where they are used: all ten Codementor React cards read 5.0, MentorCruise states "97% of exit-survey ratings are 5 stars", and a developer said it plainly — "każdy ma pięć gwiazdek". `[BENCHMARK]` https://www.codementor.io/react-experts and https://mentorcruise.com/, both checked 2026-09-04 · `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-09-01-interview-backend-dev-fintech.md`
- Senior engineers answer the same handful of questions repeatedly, unpaid, and what they write does not survive: "two evenings a month, for free, and the notes rot"; three recurring questions a week answered from a private paste doc — "ten doc, z którego wklejam. to jest całe value". `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-08-22-interview-mentor.md`, `2026-09-03-interview-senior-python-meetups.md`
- The mentor-side blocker to earning is the relationship model, not the money: a staff engineer declined MentorCruise because "nie chcę relacji. chcę odpowiedzieć na pytanie i mieć spokój", and a mentor refused invoicing outright — "faktury. nie". `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-09-03-interview-staff-engineer-declined-before.md`, `2026-09-03-interview-senior-python-meetups.md` · MentorCruise sells exactly that model: rates displayed "Starting from $100 /month" with a four-week cadence. `[BENCHMARK]` https://mentorcruise.com/, checked 2026-09-04
- Willingness to pay is stated, not demonstrated: 37 of 54 would pay something and 23 would pay 25 EUR or more, but only 3 have ever paid anyone for help. The survey also disagrees with itself by 8 people: 17 answered "nothing" to the price question while 9 answered "nobody, I would not pay" to the who-pays question, and neither the file nor its reading aid notes the gap. `[DATA]` `2026-09-04-survey-discord-willingness-to-pay.md` — an intention in a survey, not a transaction; this is the gap A01 tests
- How often this happens to one developer is not known. Founder A recorded the gap in both of her notes that raised it: "jak często to się dzieje (jeden piątek, nie wiem czy wzorzec)", and "ciągle" was never quantified. The 2026-08-20 and 2026-08-22 notes name no interviewer at all. `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-09-01-interview-backend-dev-fintech.md`, `2026-09-02-interview-freelancer-react.md` — on the collection plan

## Product and how it stands out

- What it is (1.0): a marketplace where a mentee finds a mentor, books a 25- or 50-minute live text session, and pays with their own card. `[DOCUMENT]` D05, D25
- What it is not yet: the mentor-owned vault, its subscription, MCP access and async Q&A are all `Later`. `[DOCUMENT]` D03
- What makes it different, and each difference is a decision rather than a feature: no ratings anywhere, a link to public work instead (N02, D21); text only (N01); the mentee's question is required at booking and visible to the mentor before the session (R20); no monthly plans or mentorship programs (N03); the list is ordered by most recently published availability, never by a score (D22). `[DOCUMENT]` the cited records
- What would make it different once `Later` ships: a mentor-owned knowledge layer reachable from a coding agent, with every answer citing the mentor and the exact note (D15). Competitors sell hours, monthly relationships, subscriptions and courses — the table below shows all four — but none of them sells the mentor's own body of knowledge. `[ASSUMPTION]` `2026-09-02-miro-board-product-goals.md`

## Goals and success criteria

- Primary metric: completed paid sessions per week; threshold 10 a week held for two consecutive weeks, by 2026-12-31, with 1.0 shipping 2026-10-31. `[DOCUMENT]` D06
- Baseline today is 0, and it is a true zero: the repository holds a bootstrap scaffold only — `GET /api/health`, `GET`/`POST /api/users`, an `/admin/users` page and one `mentor-profile` entity, with no booking, payment or session code and no users. `[PRODUCT]` `packages/app/src/app/`, `packages/db/src/entities/`
- Warning line: fewer than 3 paid sessions in the fourth week after ship stops feature work and starts interviews with developers who visited a mentor page and did not book. `[DOCUMENT]` D16
- Mentor supply is a gate, not the metric: at least 5 of the first 20 invited must publish a bookable session within two weeks of accepting, or invitations stop until the decliners have been interviewed. `[DOCUMENT]` D17, D18
- Instrumented from day one: the time from booking to session start. A median above 48 hours in the first four weeks moves instant availability to the front of 1.1. `[DOCUMENT]` D22
- What must not get worse: the two promises that buy trust on each side — no scores about a mentor anywhere in the product (N02), and a mentee refunded the same day when a mentor does not show (R18).
- The board's year-one goals — 100 mentors with published vaults, 30% of active mentees on a subscription, every mentor earning from two tiers, subscription revenue overtaking commission by year two — carry no owner, baseline or date and are not the primary metric. `[ASSUMPTION]` `2026-09-02-miro-board-product-goals.md`

## Scope

- **Now (1.0, ships 2026-10-31):** a mentor accepts an invitation, builds a page from a link to their public work, a plain description and stack tags, and publishes 25- and 50-minute prices within the operator's bounds plus bookable availability. A mentee finds that page (via the mentor's share link or a list ordered by most recently published availability), writes the question the booking requires, pays with their own card through Stripe Checkout for a slot at least two hours out, and holds a live text session with both people present in an in-product session screen — a text thread bound to the booked slot, with no external channel (D30). Prepayment forfeits on a mentee no-show or a late cancellation; a mentor no-show is refunded by hand the same day. The mentor supplies payout details only before their first payout, and in the first iteration the operator pays them by hand by bank transfer, logged in the shared operator note; Stripe Connect arrives in 1.1, before the second batch of invitations (R05 as amended). Terms for both roles are published before the first real payment moves. 1.0 also has to build what the operator uses: the price bounds behind R08, a commission rate the operator can change (R19), and the shared operator note where every manual refund and every manual payout is logged (R18, R05 as amended). Invitations do not go out cold: each carries a warm intro from a founder and a named developer from the founders' network with a question ready, so a mentor can see a first booking before or just after publishing (R17 as amended).
- **Later:** the mentor-owned vault, its subscription and MCP access, and async Q&A — the vault returns only if at least 3 of 10 mentee interviews (E00-T03, readout 2026-11-30) show willingness to pay for a named mentor's notes (D03). Stripe Connect onboarding and automated payouts (1.1, R05 as amended); automated refunds and dispute handling; invoicing, purchase orders and company details (D25); instant availability, promoted to the front of 1.1 if the booking-to-start median exceeds 48 hours (D22).
- **Not doing:** see Non-goals — N01 video, N02 ratings, N03 mentorship programs and monthly plans, N04 community, N05 learning paths.
- 1.0 serves the same-day blocker, not the twenty-minute one. A slot starts at least two hours after booking (D26), the copy promises no speed (D21), and the product records booking-to-start (D22). The fintech developer whose condition was "tylko żebym go miał w dwadzieścia minut" is a 1.1 customer, not a 1.0 one — accepted deliberately rather than overlooked. `[DOCUMENT]` this session
- The 25 hours from the workshop are the first iteration's build budget, not the budget for all of 1.0. `[DOCUMENT]` D14
- What 1.0 must add to the repository, and what it must not break: the protected surfaces today are internal only — package `exports` maps, the `/api/*` response envelope, and the database, where a migration that has run cannot be undone by editing code. Booking, payment and session tables are new surfaces to add to that file in the same PR, and so are three the Stripe work will touch: adding a required environment variable with no default is breaking (§4, so the Stripe keys are), a Stripe Checkout webhook is a brand-new external surface its preamble says must be added the moment it appears, and event ids such as `bookings.booking.created` are governed by §5. `[PRODUCT]` `BACKWARD_COMPATIBILITY.md`

## Key flows

- **Current state — a developer looks for help (the pain 1.0 replaces):** the problem appears in the IDE; search and docs; ask friends, Discord, LinkedIn; try to judge a stranger's competence from a profile and GitHub; message and wait; exchange times; agree a payment method; a call that starts with explaining context; afterwards nothing is kept. The stages restate the mentee interview; the thoughts and emotions on the board are the founders' reconstruction. `[INTERVIEW]` `2026-08-20-interview-working-dev.md` · `[ASSUMPTION]` `2026-09-02-miro-board-journey-current-state.md`

## Kill criteria

One review on 2026-12-31, called by both founders, with three defined outcomes and no fourth:

- **Works** — 10 completed paid sessions a week held for two consecutive weeks. `[DOCUMENT]` D06
- **Stops** — no single week reached 3 completed paid sessions **while at least 5 mentors were bookable**; or fewer than 5 mentors were ever bookable at all, which means supply failed the D18 check and did not recover. `[DOCUMENT]` D28
- **Extends once** — anything in between: some week above 3, never 10 twice running. Eight more weeks with exactly two named changes — the second batch of 20 invitations, and instant availability if the booking-to-start median is above 48 hours — and the next decision on 2027-02-28. `[DOCUMENT]` D31

The supply condition is load-bearing: without it a zero could mean "nobody wants this" or "nothing was ever bookable", and those have opposite consequences. The never-bookable clause is what stops an unreadable signal from postponing the decision indefinitely.
