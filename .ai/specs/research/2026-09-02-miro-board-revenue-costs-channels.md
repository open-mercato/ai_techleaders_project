# Discovery board (Miro) — Revenue Streams, Cost Factors, Channels. Exported 2026-09-02 by founder A (Oliwia)

Document type: the founders' own discovery board; business-model positions, not evidence.

## Revenue Streams
- Session commission: 15–20% of every booked 25/50-min session.
- Vault subscriptions: 20–30% platform cut of each mentor's monthly vault price.
- Async Q&A: platform cut of each paid question, or bundled into vault tiers.
- DevMentor membership: student pays monthly for MCP access and cross-mentor search, includes N questions.
- Mentor Pro: mentors pay monthly for analytics, priority in search, custom vault domain.
- Session packs: prepaid credits at discount, cash upfront.
- Team plans: companies buy session and vault credits for their engineers, priced per seat.
- Featured placement: mentors pay for higher visibility in search and quick filters.
- Later: combined vault and AI synthesis as premium tier with revenue share.

## Cost Factors
- Develop: engineering time for marketplace, booking, payments, vault, MCP.
- Develop: LLM inference for vault answers, scales with questions asked.
- Develop: hosting, database, storage for notes, video/meeting links.
- Develop: payment provider fees (Stripe ~3% + payout costs to mentors).
- Market: mentor acquisition, first 100 mentors need outreach, onboarding, incentives.
- Market: developer acquisition, content, SEO, community presence, dev conferences.
- Market: brand and trust, reviews, case studies, mentor spotlights.
- Sell: free trials and credits to get first bookings and subscriptions.
- Sell: later, sales effort for team plans.
- Service: support for booking disputes, no-shows, refunds, SLA misses.
- Service: content moderation and approval process for vault notes.
- Service: mentor payouts, tax and invoicing across countries.
- Service: legal — terms, IP ownership of notes, GDPR, recordings consent.

## Channels
- Mentors first: hand-pick 50 senior devs from Brave Courses, conference speakers, tech Twitter/LinkedIn, invite-only.
- Mentors bring users: every mentor gets a vault landing page and share links, their audience is the first customer base.
- Developer channels: dev communities (10xDevs, Mega Dev, AITL), newsletters, YouTube devs as mentors.
- Content: mentor vault previews as public SEO content, "how X solved Y" posts.
- Product-led: MCP integration listed in Claude Code / Cursor / MCP directories, discovered from inside the tools.
- Referrals: free question or session credit for inviting a colleague.
- Partnerships: bootcamps and course platforms like Brave Courses as follow-up mentoring.
- Team sales later: direct outreach to eng managers, start from mentors' own employers.
- Channels exist today: yes, all of them, none require building a new channel.
- Channel that's new: MCP directories, still small but growing, low competition.
- Biggest risk: chicken-and-egg, mentors won't publish without students, so seed with mentors who already have audiences.
