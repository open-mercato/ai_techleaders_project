# Narrative subject: paid text session
This describes proposed product behaviour, not observed screens or working software.
1. An operator invites a mentor. The mentor signs in via GitHub or email/password, provides a description, a public-work link and stack tags, sets prices for 25/50 minutes in PLN (operator bounds 90–600 /180–1200), and publishes availability. No open mentor registration. An invitation requests at least one bookable session within two weeks. The mentor shares a profile link.
2. A developer visits that link or a mentor list filtered by stack. It is ordered by recent published availability. Public work and descriptions are the trust information; no ratings or score ranking. GitHub is the first sign-in option; email/password is the fallback.
3. The developer selects a 25- or 50-minute session at the stated mentor price. A slot starts at least two hours after booking. The product promises no immediate answer. The developer signs in as needed and pays through Stripe Checkout. Only verified payment confirms the booking. Free cancellation is available until 24 hours before the session; later cancellation forfeits the fee. Quality disputes go to the operator; there is no promised automatic refund for a disputed outcome.
4. At the booked time the parties have a text session. The proposed channel is a session screen in DevMentor; the current refresh still awaits the owner's reconfirmation. Video is excluded. The product says to share only material the developer is allowed to share; it does not check ownership. A written answer remains available after the session. The narrative does not specify a question-before-booking form, no-show settlement or employer approval flow.
5. The mentor drafts a note. The developer approves it in full or declines with a comment; the mentor can redraft. The approved note stays private to both parties. No vault publication, subscriptions, MCP or AI drafting in this release. Payouts use Stripe Connect and a 20% platform fee. The order of onboarding versus the first paid booking is currently an unresolved product decision; do not invent its resolution.
